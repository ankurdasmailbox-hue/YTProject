Three point nine billion years ago, deep in the frozen outskirts of the newborn solar system, an invisible disaster was unfolding. Jupiter and Saturn, swollen with cosmic gas, were spiraling into a deadly gravitational dance. As their orbits locked into a catastrophic two-to-one resonance, their combined gravity tore through the outer asteroid belts like a cosmic wrecking ball. Millions of frozen planetesimals, comets, and mountain-sized asteroids were ripped from their quiet orbits and hurled directly into the inner solar system. Earth, just beginning to heal its cooling crust, stood directly in the crosshairs of an astronomical firing squad.

Act 2: THE SCARRED WITNESS: THE LUNAR ARCHIVE

00:46 - 01:38 (52s)

Camera: lunar_surface_crater_zoom | Mood: forensic_lunar_mystery

Earth remembers almost nothing of this nightmare. Tectonic churning, oceans, and weather wiped our ancient crime scene completely clean. To see what happened to our planet, look at our closest neighbor: the Moon. When Apollo astronauts landed in the lunar highlands and returned with impact melt rocks from the Imbrium and Serenitatis basins, isotope geochemists discovered an astonishing pattern. Nearly every single shock-melted rock was dated to exactly the same narrow window: 3.9 billion years ago. Scientists called it the Late Heavy Bombardment: a sudden, violent cataclysm that scarred the Moon with craters the size of entire nations. And whatever struck the Moon... hit Earth with ten times the fury.

For forty years, science accepted this story as fact: a single, sudden spike of astronomical violence that nearly obliterated the planet. But in recent years, a fierce scientific debate has fractured the consensus. Geochemists re-analyzing Apollo sample data and ancient terrestrial zircons noticed something troubling. Many of the lunar impact ages might have been biased by samples originating from just one gigantic basin: the Imbrium impact, which scattered its melted debris across half the lunar face. Dynamic models now suggest the bombardment might not have been a sudden spike at all, but rather an exponentially decaying tail of planet formation stretching over hundreds of millions of years. Was it a single, terrifying cataclysm? Or a grueling, half-billion-year gauntlet that infant Earth had to endure?

Whichever theory is correct, the central mystery remains: Did this planetary bombardment sterilize the planet? Did it reset Earth's clock back to zero? In 2009, geophysicists Oleg Abramov and Stephen Mojzsis ran complex three-dimensional thermal simulations of Earth's crust under the heaviest possible impact flux. Their results overturned everything. Even during the peak bombardment, less than twenty-five percent of Earth's crust ever melted simultaneously. Thousands of meters beneath the boiling seas, in fractured basalt and deep hydrothermal aquifers, temperatures remained cool enough for hyperthermophilic microbes to thrive. The surface was hell. But deep underground, Earth's first living organisms were safely insulated inside an indestructible rocky bunker.
